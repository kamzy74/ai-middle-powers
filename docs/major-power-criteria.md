**AI Middle Powers: Defining the Major Power Ceiling**

19 November 2026 · Dr Chris Agape Ajah

* # **Purpose**

This note adds a major power category to the scoring plan, defined on all 20 criteria across the five dimensions. It is the ceiling Erik asked for, sitting above the strong middle power tier.

The category has to meet two tests:

•      **It uses the same framework.** A major power is scored on the same 20 criteria as a middle power, at a higher threshold, so the two tiers sit on one scale.

•      **It is open.** Any country that reaches the major power level on enough criteria enters the category, whether or not it is named today. No single criterion, including a permanent Security Council seat, is required.

Working position (keep in view): the US and China are the only major AI powers today. The pilot below confirms this on data: the US meets the major power level on all 20 criteria and China on 18 to 20, while no other actor scores above 10, even counting every borderline case.

# **Working definition**

A major AI power is a country that can develop and deploy frontier AI across the full stack without depending on a rival power, and that sets the terms of AI development and governance for other countries.

The five dimensions separate the two tiers in the same way: middle powers hold weight and use it through others, while major powers hold weight at global scale and use it on their own.

| Dimension | Middle power | Major power |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| A. Economic and material weight | Large enough to fund AI and matter in trade talks | Holds a tenth or more of the world's economy, market and talent |
| B. AI and compute capacity | Holds one or two inputs others need | Holds most of the stack, and can deny its inputs to others |
| C. Diplomatic and institutional leverage | Convenes, bridges and builds coalitions | Authors the agendas and frameworks others convene around |
| D. Security and strategic weight | Brings security credibility to the table | Holds global-scale deterrence and negotiates directly with other major powers |
| E. AI governance capacity and engagement | Participates in and adopts international rules | Writes rules that reach beyond its borders and tests other countries' models |

# **How the major power level works**

Each of the 20 criteria now has two thresholds: the middle power measure already in the criteria list, and a higher major power level. A country is scored at both levels from the same data.

•      **Quantitative criteria (1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 16\)** use a share of the world total, with 10% as the major power line. A share can be reached by any country, and by more than one at a time. The precedent is Modelski and Thompson (1988), who judge global reach by a state's share of world capability.

•      **Qualitative criteria (2, 5, 10 to 15, 17 to 20\)** move from taking part to setting terms. A middle power joins, convenes or adopts; a major power authors, enforces or is negotiated with directly.

The major power tier is added to the top of the existing scoring table:

| Tier | Score out of 20 | Extra condition |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Major power** | **16 or more at the major power level** | **At least half the criteria in every dimension at the major power level** (A: 2 of 4, B: 3 of 5, C: 3 of 5, D: 2 of 3, E: 2 of 3\) |
| Strong middle power | 13 or more | Scores in at least 4 of the 5 dimensions |
| Weak middle power | 8 to 12 | Scores in at least 3 of the 5 dimensions |
| Not a middle power | 7 or fewer | None |

* The major power test runs first; every other country goes on to the middle power score. The 16-point line lets a major power miss a few criteria on weak data, while the per-dimension condition stops a country qualifying on one strength alone, such as regulation or military weight.

# **Keeping the category open over time**

Four rules let a new country enter, or an existing one leave, without redesigning the framework.

•      **Fixed sources, annual refresh.** Use 3-year rolling averages for the quantitative criteria so a single large investment year does not move a country.

•      **Two consecutive assessments to enter or leave.** This stops countries flipping between tiers.

•      **An "emerging major power" watch tier.** For countries at the major power level on 12 to 15 criteria. This flags a rising power early.

•      **Compute counted by control, not location.** Hosted clusters count toward criterion 6 at the middle power level; only compute under domestic control counts at the major power level.

## **What the pilot shows**

1\.   **The two tiers separate cleanly.** Major powers score 18 or more; the best-placed middle powers score 5 or 6 on firm evidence.

2\.   **China's status does not rest on any one indicator.** Compute and capital are borderline, but China is at the major power level on 18 criteria and in every dimension.

3\.   **Dimensions C and E show the difference between convening and authoring.** The UK, Japan and France score most of their points here, through summits and codes of conduct, which is the middle power role the literature describes. They do not reach the major power level in Dimensions A, B or D.

4\.   **Russia scores 4 to 5\.** A permanent seat and the largest arsenal do not make it a major AI power.

5\.   **The EU is a market and regulatory power.** It is strong in Dimensions A and E but has no criteria at the major power level in Dimension D, so it fails the per-dimension condition however the borderline cases fall.

6\.   **India is the case to watch.** It has no criteria firmly at the major power level today, but its convening, coalition and governance record puts five criteria at the borderline.

# **Fit with the scoring plan and next steps**

The major power test uses the same 20 criteria and the same data as the middle power score, so one round of data collection serves both tiers. It also answers two open questions from the criteria list:

•      **Nuclear status counts toward both tiers, at different levels.** Holding nuclear weapons earns the middle power point; an arsenal of 500 or more earns the major power point. This is why the UK, France and India score at the middle power level but not above it.

•      **The EU can be scored as a bloc.** It reaches 10 at most and has no criteria at the major power level in Dimension D, so treating it as one actor does not create a third major power.
